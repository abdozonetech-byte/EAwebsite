Act as an instructional designer.

Task: Give constructive learner feedback.

02 — Example Result

A concrete example to review.

An editorial illustrative example, not a model-tested or guaranteed result. It does not change when you edit variables.

Feedback pattern

“Your comparison identifies the two sources clearly. The conclusion needs a link to a specific passage. Choose one claim, cite its supporting section and explain the limitation.” Tie comments to actual work rather than assumed motivation. This is an illustrative feedback draft and does not assess an identifiable learner or establish a grade.

What you get

Ground feedback in the supplied work. Identify one strength, one priority improvement and an actionable next step. Use respectful language, avoid unsupported personal inferences and do not replace teacher judgement in consequential assessment.

When to use it

  • Prepare a reviewable deliverable: give constructive learner feedback.
  • Compare assumptions, evidence and practical next steps before taking action.

Professional tips

  • Provide concrete, authorised context for work sample before using this prompt.
  • Keep useful constraints when iterating and verify important claims independently.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving essential context ambiguous while expecting a precise result.
  • Treating the illustrative example as a verified result or evidence of model testing.
